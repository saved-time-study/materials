# The session, page by page

This file gives every page that each of the four groups sees, in order, with the time at which it appears and its
exact text. Words in quotation marks and the answer options of each question are the words on the screen. The
consent and debriefing texts are the drafts submitted for ethics review, and the approved versions replace them
before data collection. In the countries whose main language is not English, participants see the same pages in
translation (the Methods describe how the translations are made and checked).

## The plan they review

One dinner plan, the same for every participant, drafted by a generative AI assistant before data collection and checked
by a research assistant: dinners for Monday to Sunday (seven sections) and a shopping list in six sections (produce,
meat and fish, dairy, bakery, pantry, frozen), 13 sections in all. "Section" always names a piece of the plan, and
"part" always names a part of the session. Amounts use the units of each country (cups and degrees Fahrenheit in the
United States; grams, millilitres and degrees Celsius elsewhere), and the translators adapt the names of foods and the
sections of the shopping list for each country. Each section fills one page that moves on by itself after 60 seconds;
there is no Next button and no timer is shown. A counter above the section reads "Section 1 of 3" in the planned group
and "Section 1 of 13" in the other groups, so each group sees again on every page what it was told at the start. The
participant can edit the section in an editable box ("Edit anything you want to change. This page moves on by itself
after one minute."). The session records, for sections 1 to 3, the number of edits, the characters changed and whether
the page was the visible tab, and reports them by group.

## Before assignment (the same for everyone)

1. Consent (the text that the ethics board approves). The draft says: "This study has a review of a dinner plan drafted by an AI
   assistant, other short activities, and a few questions. It takes about 17 minutes. Some details of the study will be
   explained at the end." It names no free time and no break.
2. Attention check: "Which of these is a day of the week?" Tuesday / Purple / Seven / Kitchen. A participant who does
   not choose Tuesday ends the session there, is paid for the minute spent, and is not assigned.
3. "How often do you use generative AI tools such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude or Copilot?" Never / Less than once a week
   / 1 to 6 days a week / Every day. ("Weekly" users are the last two answers.)
4. "Please keep this window open until the end." [Start]. The click on Start assigns the group, in permuted blocks of
   four within each country.

## The timeline, side by side

Screen 1 loads. All times run from c. [clock time] is c + 14:00 in the participant's local time and usual format ("3:47
p.m." in the United States, "15:47" in continental Europe and Latin America), and the questions open within the minute
it names. From section 1 until the questions, a fixed header on every page reads "The questions open at [clock time]."

The groups differ in three places: what Screen 1 says (a review of three sections, a 10-minute break with its reason,
and pay; or a review of 13 sections), the counter on sections 1 to 3 ("of 3" or "of 13"), which repeats what Screen 1
said, and the first sentence of the message at c + 3:00 (why the review ends), to which the labeled group's message adds
"That saved you 10 minutes." Every other line is the same in every group, including the sentence on pay and the start
time of the break at c + 3:00. Between the found-break and AI-saved groups the message differs only in whether the
remaining review was done and by whom: it "will not be reviewed in this session" (dropped), or "the AI assistant has
reviewed" it (completed). Neither sentence says anything about the quality of the remaining sections. This is the
difference that H2 and H5 test.

## Choosing (c + 3:00 to c + 4:00, one page, the same for every group)

The page shows the message, then two steps:
1. The pick, as in Chung and colleagues' fifth study: "Pick five videos to watch during your break. Each is about two
   minutes long." The ten videos of their study 5, each as a tile with its title (and a thumbnail if their materials
   have one), in one random order per person, with no category labels. The work option is not shown until five videos
   are picked, so the pick is made as in the original study.
2. When five videos are picked, the slots appear under the tiles: "You can use any of your five 2-minute slots to earn
   extra money instead. Your videos play in a random order, so a slot you use to earn extra replaces one of your videos
   at random." For each of Slot 1 to Slot 5: (o) Watch a video ( ) Earn extra: four short typing items, 0.10 USD each.
   Below the slots: "Or leave the session now, with full pay." [Leave now]. Choosing Leave now takes the participant to
   the questions; it cannot be combined with paid slots, and the minutes given to paid extra work are 0.

The page records the seconds spent before the fifth pick and before the last change of the slots. The break starts at
c + 4:00 for everyone; a participant who has finished sees "Your break starts at [time]." At c + 4:00:
- Slots not changed play a video, as their default shows.
- Picks still missing are filled at random so that the slots have videos to play, but filled picks are recorded and do
  not count: the share of fun videos is computed over the picks the participant made. A participant who made no pick
  has no value for the share of fun and keeps every other outcome.
- The share of participants who had not finished each step at c + 4:00 is reported by group, and H1, H4 and H5 are
  repeated, as a robustness check, among participants who made all five picks themselves.

## The 10-minute break (c + 4:00 to c + 14:00)

| Slot | What happens |
|---|---|
| A video slot | The video plays (about 2 minutes). In countries whose main language is not English, it plays with subtitles in that language. |
| A work slot | Four transcription items, each lasting exactly 30 seconds: a short handwritten note to type. An item pays 0.10 USD when at least 90 percent of its characters are typed correctly, scored by the program; capitals and punctuation are ignored, and a letter typed without its accent counts as correct. Earnings show under the items. Notes are written in the language of the materials and matched in characters across languages (within 10 percent of the English notes). Four items a slot pay at most 0.40 USD, a rate of 0.20 USD a minute. |
| Each slot | Lasts 2 minutes whatever the participant does in it. |

The session records, for every second of the break, whether the page is the visible tab. Hidden time is computed over
the minutes a participant gave to videos and stayed for; it has no value for a participant who left or gave every slot
to work.

## The questions (from c + 14:00, about 2 minutes; leavers see them right after choosing Leave now)

1. Chung and colleagues' two items, 1 to 7: "How unexpected was it to have the 10-minute break?" and "How surprising was
   it to have the 10-minute break?" (Not at all, 1, to Very much, 7.) The score is their mean.
2. "What do you think this study was about?" (free text, coded blind to group into whether it names the reason the
   review ended or the source of the break; reported by group and by week of data collection).
3. "Before today, had you read or heard anything about this study?" Yes / No / Not sure.
4. "How did the review of the plan end?" As planned, after three sections / The remaining sections would not be reviewed
   / The AI assistant reviewed the remaining sections / I don't know. The full table of answers is reported by group.
5. "Which of these sentences did you see during the session? Choose all that apply." "You have reviewed the three
   sections." / "The remaining sections of the plan will not be reviewed in this session." / "The AI assistant has
   reviewed the remaining sections of the plan itself." / "That saved you 10 minutes." / "Your review was saved
   automatically." / "The session will restart in one minute." (The last two were seen by no group.)
6. The debriefing (below), then the return to Prolific.

Each item is required, so the analysis has no missing answers. Items 2 and 3 come before items 4 and 5, which show every
group's reason, so the guesses about the study's purpose are made before participants see the other groups' messages.

## Consent and debriefing (drafts for ethics review)

The study uses incomplete disclosure: the found-break and both AI-saved groups are told that they will review 13
sections, and the review ends after 3. The consent (above) names no free time, so it is true for every group. Every
participant, leavers included, reads this debriefing before returning to Prolific:

"Thank you for taking part. This study asks what people do with free time, depending on how the time came to them.
Participants were placed at random in one of four groups. In one group, the break was planned from the start. In the
other three, people were told that they would review all 13 sections of the plan, and the review ended after 3, either
because the rest would not be reviewed or because the AI assistant had reviewed it. The plan, the length of the review
and the reason it ended were set by the study; no AI reviewed your plan during the session. You receive your full pay
whatever you chose, and any typing items that met the scoring rule are paid as a bonus. Please do not tell other
Prolific users how the study works, because it would change what they do. If you do not want your answers used, you can
withdraw them now, and you will still be paid in full." [Keep my answers / Withdraw my answers]

A withdrawn participant is not analyzed and is replaced, like a participant who does not finish. A table by group and
country gives the participants assigned, not finishing, leaving early and withdrawing.

## Session length and pay

From the consent to the return to Prolific, the session takes about 17 minutes: about 30 seconds before assignment, 30
seconds on Screen 1, 14 minutes from c to the questions, and about 2 minutes for the questions and the debriefing.
At a rate of 0.15 USD a minute, 17 minutes pay 2.55 USD.
