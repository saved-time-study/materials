Written by figures/make_captions.py, which reads every number from the results files; do not edit this file by hand. Each legend is followed by the file and key of each of its numbers, for checking; that list is not part of the legend.

**Figure 1. In US time diaries, the gaps in leisure and paid work between the most and least exposed workers in 2023 to 2025 do not differ from 2019 (a), and the estimates of the model of Jiang and colleagues, as we rebuilt it, depend on the baseline year (b).** **a**, Most minus least exposed third of workers (GPT-4 exposure rating of Eloundou and colleagues), in daily minutes against 2019 (diamond), from a regression with occupation and year fixed effects (American Time Use Survey, N = 30,933 diaries of the two outer thirds, 258 occupations; the middle third and 2020 are left out). Marks are blue for 2015 to 2019, orange for 2022, the baseline year of Jiang and colleagues, and black for 2021 and 2023 to 2025. Lines are 95% confidence intervals clustered by occupation. The dashed line marks the release of ChatGPT on 30 November 2022. **b**, The change in weekly hours at the 75th against the 25th percentile of exposure in the model of Jiang and colleagues, against 2022 (orange) or 2015 to 2019 (blue). The hollow circle is their published estimate for 2023 (work 3.15 hours, t = 2.12; leisure −3.20 hours, t = −1.92), with a 95% confidence interval computed from the published estimate and its t statistic. Filled circles are the estimates of their model as we rebuilt it (N = 7,937, 14,286, 30,503 and 32,869 diaries, left to right), with 95% confidence intervals clustered by occupation and state. Work and leisure in **b** are their measures, not those of **a**, and their leisure includes sleep and personal care above 7 hours a day.

**Figure 2. The four groups share one timeline and differ in what they are told at the start and in what they read when the review ends.** **a**, Every group reviews three sections, chooses for 1 minute, has a 10-minute break and answers questions for about 2 minutes (to scale). On Screen 1, the first page after assignment, the planned-break group is told of three sections and then a break, and the other groups are told of all 13 sections. After three sections, each group reads why the review ends, and the labeled group also reads “That saved you 10 minutes.” **b**, In the minute of choosing, participants pick five of ten videos, shown without category labels (the colors of the tiles are for the reader only). They can then give any 2-minute slot to paid typing, or leave with full pay. The example's outcomes are a share of fun videos of 60 percent and 4 minutes of paid extra work. Text in quotation marks is screen text.

**Figure 3. Under each of four patterns of true differences, each registered test gives the result that the pattern implies in about 96% of simulated data sets.** **a**, For each hypothesis, the predicted means of its two groups (left to right) under four patterns. In the first row (windfall account), time saved by AI is a windfall, spent like found time. In the second (work account), time saved by AI still belongs to work and is therefore spent on fun like planned time, given back to paid work and spent on less fun than found time. In the third (all hypotheses true), H1, H2 and H5 are each true at d = 0.15, the smallest effect of interest, H3 at a difference of zero, and the label raises the share of fun by d = 0.15 (H4). In the fourth (no difference between groups), no group differs. Rising and falling lines are differences of d = 0.15. **b**, The result that each pattern implies and its share of 20,000 simulated data sets, each of 1,706 analyzable participants a group in 12 countries (95% Monte Carlo intervals within 0.3 percentage points). Each test uses alpha = 0.01 (0.05 divided among five comparisons) and equivalence bounds of plus or minus 0.15 standard deviations of the outcome, computed from each data set. Each tile shows the result for the hypothesis's two groups, which is higher or lower when the difference is significant and equivalent when the 98% interval lies inside the bounds. A higher result supports H1 and H2, a lower result supports H5, an equivalent result supports H3, and H4 is interpreted in either direction. In every cell, no other result occurred in more than 3.0% of data sets, and no result other than the implied one and unresolved occurred in more than 1.3%. A failed outcome-neutral criterion would count as a miss. No criterion failed in any data set.
