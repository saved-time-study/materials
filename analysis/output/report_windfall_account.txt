   H1. Do people spend a found break on more fun than a planned break?
      found_break minus planned_break, fun_share (n = 6807): 3.01 points (SE 0.99); 99% interval [0.47, 5.55], two-sided p = 0.0023.
      Equivalence against plus or minus 4.37 points: 98% interval [0.72, 5.30], p = 0.0835.
      Reading: SUPPORTED. A found break goes to more fun than a planned break: the found-time effect of Chung and colleagues, in the pooled sample of 12 countries.
   H2. Do people give AI-saved time to paid work more, or less, than found time?
      ai_saved minus found_break, paid_work_minutes (n = 6824): 0.18 minutes (SE 0.10); 99% interval [-0.08, 0.44], two-sided p = 0.0738.
      Equivalence against plus or minus 0.45 minutes: 98% interval [-0.05, 0.42], p = 0.0046.
      Reading: EQUIVALENT. AI-saved and found time go to paid work alike, within the bound, as the windfall account predicts.
   H3. Do people spend AI-saved time on fun like a planned break?

      Reading: HIGHER THAN PLANNED. AI-saved time goes to more fun than planned time, as the windfall account predicts; H5 shows whether it goes to as much fun as found time.
