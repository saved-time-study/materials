   H1. Do people spend a found break on more fun than a planned break?

      Equivalence against plus or minus 4.40 points: 98% interval [1.83, 6.47], p = 0.4002.
      Reading: SUPPORTED. A found break goes to more fun than a planned break: the found-time effect of Chung and colleagues, in the pooled sample of 12 countries.
   H2. Do people give AI-saved time to paid work more, or less, than found time?
      ai_saved minus found_break, paid_work_minutes (n = 6824): 0.39 minutes (SE 0.11); 99% interval [0.12, 0.66], two-sided p = 0.0002.
      Equivalence against plus or minus 0.46 minutes: 98% interval [0.15, 0.64], p = 0.2530.
      Reading: SUPPORTED. The work account: time saved when an assistant completes the work goes back to paid work more than time freed because the work was dropped.
   H3. Do people spend AI-saved time on fun like a planned break?
      ai_saved minus planned_break, fun_share (n = 6804): 0.80 points (SE 1.00); 99% interval [-1.77, 3.38], two-sided p = 0.4222.
      Equivalence against plus or minus 4.42 points: 98% interval [-1.52, 3.13], p = 0.0002.
      Reading: SUPPORTED. AI-saved time goes to fun like planned time, within the bound, as the work account predicts.
   H4. Does labeling the saving change the share of fun videos, and in which direction?
